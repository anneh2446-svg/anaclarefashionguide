Fabric guide

Which fabrics actually work best in hot weather?

Heat, humidity, sweat and direct sun all change how clothing feels — a shirt that's fine indoors can turn heavy outside. Here's how to choose fabric, fit and sun protection for whatever your day actually involves.

The basics

What makes a fabric good for hot weather?

The right warm-weather fabric should feel comfortable against your skin and allow enough airflow for the activity. Fabric is only one part of comfort — the way a garment is cut can matter just as much. A loose dress lets air move around the body; a fitted performance top stays in place during sport.

Why it matters

Breathability matters most when temperatures rise

A breathable garment allows heat and moisture to move away more easily, which can make a long day outdoors feel far more manageable. The goal isn't simply the thinnest fabric you can find — a well-designed garment can feel comfortable even when it isn't extremely thin.

Fabric by fabric

Cotton, linen or synthetic — how to choose

There's no single "best" fabric. Each one trades off differently between softness, airflow, drying speed and care, so the right pick depends on what your day looks like.

Casual

Cotton

Soft and familiar, cotton works well for casual summer outfits. Its one limitation: it absorbs moisture. If you sweat heavily, cotton can become damp and take longer to dry, which makes it less comfortable for intense outdoor activity.

Best forCasual lunches, shopping, light errands, easy walks, at-home wear.
Resort

Linen & linen blends

Linen's structure allows good airflow, which makes it popular in warm climates and gives it that relaxed resort look. The main drawback is wrinkling — a linen blend can offer a better balance of airflow, comfort and easier care.

Best forVacation dressing, resort days, relaxed sightseeing.
Active

Synthetic performance fabric

Designed synthetics can offer stretch, moisture management, quick drying, durability and easy washing — useful for sports and outdoor activities. But not every synthetic feels cool: a heavy synthetic can feel warmer than a lightweight performance fabric, so always consider the actual garment.

Best forGolf, pickleball, walking, outdoor workouts.
Does fit affect heat?

Fit changes airflow and movement

Very tight clothing can feel restrictive. Very loose clothing can become inconvenient during exercise. For casual wear, relaxed silhouettes feel comfortable; for sports, a balanced fit helps you move naturally while keeping the garment in place.

  • Try clothing on and actually move: raise your arms, bend, sit, walk.
  • That gives a better idea of how a garment will feel over a full day.
  • Fabric weight matters, but cut and fit decide how it wears.
Sweat happens

Moisture management makes the difference

A fabric that becomes saturated can feel heavy and cling to the body. Moisture-managing performance fabrics are designed to handle sweat more efficiently — a real advantage on days that call for it. For casual activities, the difference is usually less noticeable.

  • Outdoor sports and golf rounds
  • Long walks and warm-weather workouts
  • Travel days spent mostly outside
Sun protection

What is UPF, and can it be worn every day?

UPF stands for Ultraviolet Protection Factor. It measures how much UV radiation passes through fabric — a tested UPF rating gives specific information about how well fabric reduces UV transmission through the covered area. A lightweight garment can feel cool with no tested rating at all, which is why it's worth treating as its own feature. You don't need to reserve UPF clothing for sports: a versatile top works just as well for gardening, travel or an outdoor errand.

It's measured, not assumed

UPF is a tested rating, not a guess based on how thick or dark a fabric looks.

Long sleeves aren't always hot

A lightweight, breathable long sleeve can stay comfortable while covering more skin during extended sun exposure.

It covers what it covers

UPF protects the skin under the fabric. Exposed skin still needs sunscreen, shade or a hat.

Common questions

Fabric questions, answered

What fabric is best for hot and humid weather?

Lightweight and breathable fabrics are generally comfortable in hot and humid conditions. Linen, lightweight cotton and suitable performance fabrics can all work well depending on the garment and activity.

Is polyester suitable for hot weather?

It can be. Lightweight performance polyester is often designed for active use and moisture management — the fabric's weight and construction make a major difference.

Is linen cooler than cotton?

Linen is well known for its airflow and airy feel. Cotton can also be comfortable, especially for relaxed activities. Your choice depends on the activity, moisture, care needs and personal preference.

Does UPF clothing replace sunscreen?

No. UPF clothing protects the skin covered by the garment. Exposed areas still need suitable protection.

Should I wear loose clothing in hot weather?

A relaxed fit can improve airflow and comfort. For active sports, though, clothing should also provide enough structure and freedom of movement.
